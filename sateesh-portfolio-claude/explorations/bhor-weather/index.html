Bhor: Weather, Decoded

A menu-bar weather app that tells you what to do, not just the numbers. Your daily dawn, decoded.

Bhor, your daily dawn, decoded
The Concept

Most weather apps hand you numbers and walk away. UV index 8. Humidity 80%. AQI 98. Fine, but what am I supposed to do with that?

Apple Weather says it's 38°. Bhor says: take an umbrella for shade, drink 3L of water, step out 9–11 AM for Vitamin D, avoid the outdoors 12–5, and watch for mosquitoes after 6 PM. Same data, different output. The difference is interpretation.

Bhor's whole idea is to never show a number without telling you what it means for your day. It reads live weather and air quality and turns it into a short list of plain-language "do this" cards, placed on a timeline of your day.

PrincipleA number is a question. An action is an answer. Bhor only ships answers.
Same data, a different answer
What It Does
Lives in the menu barGlanceable, no Dock icon, no app to "open."

Insight cardsTime-led ("9:00–11:00 AM · Vitamin D"), sorted by when they matter, with past ones dimmed.
17 rule-based insightsBest time outside, sun protection, hydration, mosquito hour, open/close windows, golden hour, and more.
Search any cityNearby-first results when you look somewhere new.
Private by designNo account, no ads, no analytics; everything stays on-device.
The Bhor popover, the day's actions at a glance
How It Works

The "brain" is a small rule engine. Each insight is a documented rule with a real threshold, e.g. Vitamin D fires when UV is in the safe synthesis band (3–8) and it isn't an "avoid outdoors" hour. The honesty matters: the rules are based on public-health and meteorological guidance, not vibes.

Data comes from free, no-key APIs (Open-Meteo for weather and air quality), so the app stays free forever and sends nothing personal anywhere.

Current Thinking

What's working. The interpretation layer is the whole point, and it lands, reading "step out before the heat" beats reading "UV 8" every time. A single menu-bar glance fits the habit better than opening an app.

Trust through consistencyEarly on the engine once said "avoid outdoors 12–7" and "sunbathe 3–5" on the same day. Contradictions quietly destroy trust, so the rules now reconcile against each other.
Personalization without clutterNext idea: pick your profession and a work location, and the same insights re-prioritize, an outdoor worker hears heat/UV loudest; a desk worker hears walk windows, but the default stays simple for everyone.
The honest limitAccurate auto-location and friction-free sharing both need Apple Developer signing. Until then, search is the reliable path, a reminder that "free + private" has real edges.
Stack & Status
Built withSwift 6 + SwiftUI, macOS 14+ (@Observable, UserDefaults, no backend). Menu-bar-only (LSUIElement), built from a written spec before any code.
DataOpen-Meteo (weather + air quality), Apple MapKit (city search), OpenStreetMap (place names), all free, no keys.
StatusA working prototype on macOS, shareable as a DMG and currently being tested with friends. iOS + Android are the natural next platforms; the rule engine ports cleanly.
