Chrome Extension · Concept

Browser Workflow Tool

A small utility experiment designed around reducing repeated browser actions and keeping everyday workflows lighter.

The Concept

Most research and reference work happens in the browser, and the browser is bad at remembering it. You open twenty tabs chasing an idea, follow one promising link into another, and three days later you can't reconstruct how you got anywhere or why a page mattered.

This exploration started from a simple frustration: your browser history is a list, but research is a tree. History flattens everything into a time-sorted log and throws away the one thing that actually carried meaning, the path between pages.

So I built a tool that captures the shape of a browsing session, not just the URLs. You start a session, browse normally, and it quietly builds a visual map: every page becomes a node, opening a link from it becomes a child, going back and branching becomes a new branch. Each node keeps a thumbnail, the page's dominant colors, and its fonts, the things a designer actually remembers a page by.

The Problem It Pokes At

The gap nobody fills: capturing the branching path of research with visual context, without asking the user to do extra work.

No recordA reference-heavy session means 40+ tabs and zero durable record of how it all connected.
BookmarksThey save URLs but lose hierarchy and context, the structure that gave the links meaning.
Notes appsThey require you to stop and transcribe. Friction kills the flow, so the record never gets made.
Tab crashesOne crash wipes an entire session, hours of branching gone in an instant.
Current Thinking
Capture must be invisibleThe moment saving requires a modal or a folder choice, people stop doing it mid-flow. The only decision should be "record / don't record", everything else is passive.
Designers think in picturesA thumbnail and a color swatch row beat a page title for recall. You remember "the dark pricing page with the orange button," never the URL.
Control beats automationAn early version auto-started itself on certain sites. It felt invasive, visiting a page shouldn't hijack your browser. The user decides when work begins.
Local-first postureEverything stays on the device. No account, no server, no telemetry. For a tool that watches your browsing, privacy isn't a feature, it's the price of trust.
What It Can Do Today
Visual treeTurns a session into a visual tree, or a sitemap view grouped by site.
Auto-captureGrabs a screenshot, color palette, and fonts for every page, automatically.
InspectSelect a single element to save just that section instead of the whole page.
ReplayPlay a session back like a slideshow to retrace the path.
ExportExport as Markdown, as an AI-ready prompt, or get an in-tool AI summary with your own key.
Open Questions

How much structure is too much before capture starts feeling like filing?

Should the map be the output, or just the raw material for a cleaner artifact, a deck, a moodboard, a brief?

Where's the line between "research tool" and "second brain"?
