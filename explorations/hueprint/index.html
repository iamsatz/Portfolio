Web App · Concept

HuePrint

Design token kits for AI-builders. Pick a brand kit, paste it into your AI tool, get branded UI, instantly.

The Concept

AI tools generate functional UI, and then paint it in the same generic blue every time. They have no "import design tokens" button; they read prompts. So the way to give an AI your brand isn't a file, it's an instruction.

HuePrint is a token-kit store built around that insight. Each kit isn't just colors, it's a design-system prompt plus tokens. The prompt tells the AI how to use the palette, not just what the colors are. Browse curated kits, preview them on real components, and download AI-ready files you paste straight into Claude, v0, Cursor, or Bolt.

One-linerPick a brand kit. Paste into your AI tool. Get branded UI.
The Problem It Pokes At
No token importAI builders don't ingest design tokens, they read prompts, so tokens alone never land.
Generic defaultsWithout guidance, AI falls back to stock colors and spacing, and everything looks the same.
"What" without "how"A hex list says what the brand is, not how to apply it across buttons, cards, and states.
Design skill gapSolo founders and indie hackers can code but can't design a coherent system from scratch.
What's In a Kit, The Moat

Each kit is a hand-curated three-file bundle:

system-prompt.mdThe AI-readable design brief, full palette (light + dark), per-component usage rules, type/spacing/radius/shadow specs, and explicit "do NOT" rules that stop AI from reverting to generic defaults.
tokens.cssshadcn/Radix-compatible CSS variables with light and dark mode, paste into globals.css and it just works.
tokens.jsonUniversal W3C DTCG format, compatible with Style Dictionary, Figma Tokens, and Tailwind config generation.
How It Works

ExportDownload the three-file bundle and paste it into any AI code tool.
Who It's For
Solo founderBuilding with Claude/v0/Cursor, no design background, wants branded UI without learning design systems.
Indie hackerShips several projects a year, codes but can't design, needs a professional, code-ready palette.
Product designerAt an early-stage startup, living in Figma + AI tools, wants fast brand exploration that exports to code.
Current Thinking & Status

The interesting design problem is the system-prompt, not the swatches. Getting an AI to apply a palette consistently, and to refuse its defaults, is more about clear usage rules and "do NOT" guardrails than about the colors themselves.

Open questionHow many rules are enough before the prompt gets too long for the AI to honour reliably?
StackReact 19 + TypeScript, Vite, Tailwind v4, shadcn/ui, chroma.js.
StatusMVP, pre-development. Scoped to 8–12 curated kits, live component preview, and the three-file export.
