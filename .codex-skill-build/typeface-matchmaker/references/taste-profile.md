# Typeface Taste Profile

This profile is distilled from the user's shared typeface bank of 844 items. The list mixes premium foundries, free/open projects, Google Fonts, Fontshare, Behance releases, Instagram discoveries, and visual references. Treat it as taste evidence, not as a licensing whitelist.

## Overall Taste

The user is drawn to typefaces that feel current, design-led, and editorially aware rather than generic. They like neutral systems when the details are tuned, display faces with strong personality, and free fonts that still feel foundry-grade.

Recurring preferences:

- Contemporary grotesks with subtle personality rather than default Helvetica mimicry.
- Geometric and technical sans families for UI, product, crypto/finance, and software contexts.
- High-contrast editorial serifs with luxury, fashion, literary, or art-book energy.
- Warm retro serifs and soft display families with Bookman/Cooper/Rekoletta-like friendliness.
- Condensed and compressed sans faces for posters, campaigns, and dense headers.
- Experimental, stencil, pixel, mono, blackletter, and script faces as accents.
- Variable fonts, large family systems, optical sizes, and robust language support.

## Recommendation Biases

When multiple options are plausible, prefer:

- Better spacing and text performance over novelty.
- Variable fonts or larger families when the project needs a system.
- A distinctive display face paired with a calmer text/UI face.
- Free fonts from reputable sources over sketchy "free download" sites.
- Alternatives that preserve the role and feeling, not superficial letterform matching.
